About

Founded by trainers tired of measuring seat-time instead of change

Open Trainings started in Noida in 2022 as a three-person facilitation practice. A few years on, it's how corporates get the three things a training budget actually needs: a trainer who's done the work, a lab to practice it in, and the certification voucher to prove it.

Where it started

The founding team had spent the previous decade delivering vendor-certified technical courses and watched the same pattern repeat: a class full of engaged learners, a certificate printed at the end, and no lab to practice in or straightforward way to sit the actual exam.

Open Trainings was built to close that loop: a vetted trainer, a working lab environment, and the certification voucher, sourced as one engagement instead of three separate vendors.

Where it is now

Delivery teams now operate out of Noida, Dubai and Nairobi, with live online delivery and travel-based in-person work extending well beyond those three cities. The trainer network has grown from technical certification prep into leadership, compliance and customer operations tracks, following demand from the same L&D teams that commissioned the early technical cohorts.

The model has stayed the same: a diagnostic before a proposal, a trainer matched to the brief, and a lab or voucher lined up wherever the programme calls for one.

How we work

Four commitments that don't change per client

01

Trainers are vetted before they're placed

Facilitators for technical and compliance tracks have worked in the field they teach, checked before the first cohort, not after a complaint.

02

Lab environments are kept current

Cloud, security and data sandboxes are rebuilt against current vendor curricula, not left running the version we set up two years ago.

03

Vouchers are sourced directly from vendors

Certification exam vouchers move through official vendor and authorised-reseller channels, never a marked-up resale.

04

Internal capacity is treated as a deliverable

Where a client wants to run future cohorts themselves, that handover is planned in from the first proposal.
